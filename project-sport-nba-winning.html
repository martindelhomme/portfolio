01 - Hypothesis

Not all stats are created equal

Every NBA broadcast drowns you in statistics. Points per game, rebounds, assists, pace, turnovers, defensive rating, the list is endless. But which of these actually explain why teams win?

The methodology mirrors factor analysis in quantitative investing: identify candidate variables, measure their explanatory power individually, then build a multi-factor model and test it for robustness.

The hypothesis going in: Net Rating and shooting efficiency will dominate. Pace, assists, and rebounds will matter far less than basketball commentary suggests.

"The question is not which stats look impressive, it's which stats actually predict wins. Correlation first, causation second."
02 - Dataset & Method

Same 300 team-seasons, more variables

Building on the dataset from Article 1 (2015-16 to 2025-26, 300 team-season observations), I added six additional variables: Defensive Rating, Pace, Assists per game, Turnovers per game, Offensive Rebound %, and Free Throw Rate. This gives 13 candidate predictors of Win%.

The analysis has three layers: single-variable correlations ranked by strength, the Dean Oliver "Four Factors" multiple regression, and a "Modern NBA" regression using the metrics that emerged as dominant.

04 - Four Factors Model

Dean Oliver's framework, tested on modern data

In 2002, basketball analyst Dean Oliver proposed that four factors explain most of team success: shooting efficiency (eFG%), turnovers (TOV%), offensive rebounding (ORB%), and getting to the free throw line (FT Rate). This was groundbreaking pre-Moneyball basketball analytics, let's see how it holds up two decades later.

Model 1 — Four Factors Regression
Win% = β₀ + β₁·eFG% + β₂·TOV% + β₃·ORB% + β₄·FTR
Coefficients estimated via OLS on 300 team-season observations. All variables normalized.
Four Factors — Individual Contributions to Win%
Scatter plots for each of Dean Oliver's four factors vs Win% | hover for team details
Finding 02
The Four Factors model explains ~37% of win% variance (R² ≈ 0.37). eFG% and TOV% carry almost all the weight. ORB% (r = 0.06) and FT Rate (r = 0.09) contribute almost nothing, Oliver's last two factors have become less predictive in the modern game.
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.metrics import r2_score
import numpy as np

# Four Factors model
features_ff = ['efg', 'tov_pct', 'orb_pct', 'ftr']
X_ff = agg[features_ff].dropna()
y_ff = agg.loc[X_ff.index, 'win_pct']

scaler = StandardScaler()
X_ff_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X_ff)

model_ff = LinearRegression().fit(X_ff_scaled, y_ff)
r2_ff = r2_score(y_ff, model_ff.predict(X_ff_scaled))

print(f"Four Factors R² = {r2_ff:.3f}")
for feat, coef in zip(features_ff, model_ff.coef_):
    print(f"  {feat}: β = {coef:.4f}")

# Modern NBA model
features_mod = ['net_rtg', 'efg', 'pace', 'apg']
X_mod = agg[features_mod].dropna()
y_mod = agg.loc[X_mod.index, 'win_pct']

X_mod_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X_mod)
model_mod = LinearRegression().fit(X_mod_scaled, y_mod)
r2_mod = r2_score(y_mod, model_mod.predict(X_mod_scaled))

06 - Limits

What this analysis cannot tell us

Correlation vs causation. Net Rating predicts wins almost perfectly, but that's partly tautological. A team's Net Rating is computed from the same games as their win percentage. It's a measure of what happened, not a predictor of what will happen.

Multicollinearity. Many variables are highly correlated with each other (eFG% and TS%, Off Rating and Net Rating). Multiple regression coefficients should be interpreted with care, individual betas are not stable when predictors are correlated.

Playoffs vs regular season. This analysis covers regular season only. The variables that predict playoff success may differ significantly, small sample sizes, opponent quality, and home court changes the dynamics entirely. Article 3 addresses this directly.

Missing variables. Clutch performance, health/injuries, schedule strength, and coaching quality are not captured here. The model explains the average, not the outliers.

07 - Conclusion
The hierarchy of NBA winning metrics
  • Net Rating (r = 0.965): The only near-perfect predictor. Score more than you allow per 100 possessions, and you will win. Everything else is downstream of this.
  • Offensive Rating, eFG%, TS% (r ≈ 0.56–0.61): The second tier. Shooting efficiency is the most controllable driver of offensive quality.
  • Defensive Rating (r = -0.51) — Defense matters, but less than offense in explaining win% variation across the league.
  • TOV% / Turnovers per game (r ≈ -0.37): Meaningful but not dominant. The Four Factors overweighted this variable relative to its actual predictive power.
  • Pace, ORB%, FT Rate, Assists (r < 0.25): Noisy. These matter in specific systems or matchups but explain little of the overall variance in wins.
  • The Four Factors model (R² ≈ 0.37) is useful but outdated, ORB% and FT Rate have lost much of their predictive power in the modern game. A two-factor model of eFG% and TOV% performs almost as well.
