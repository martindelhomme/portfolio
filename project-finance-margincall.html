Margin Call Automation
Securities Lending & Repo

Centralising and automating a critical daily risk workflow: from a fragmented manual process to a reliable morning routine.

01 - Context

A critical daily risk process running on manual work

At CACEIS, the front office manages securities lending and repo activity across multiple brokers. In exchange for lent securities, collateral is received in the form of cash or securities. Every morning, all collateral positions across approximately 35 brokers needed to be valued to assess exposure versus the securities lent, determining whether we were over or under collateralised, and whether a margin call was required.

This process was critical from a risk perspective. Incorrect or delayed valuation could lead to unhedged exposure in the event of a counterparty default.

Why it matters: Historical events such as the Lehman Brothers collapse underlined the importance of accurate and timely collateral monitoring, particularly in stressed environments like COVID, where market moves can rapidly shift collateral values and expose firms to unexpected counterparty risk.
02 - Objective

Fast, accurate and early - every morning

The objective was to ensure that daily exposure calculations and margin calls were produced quickly, accurately, and early in the morning, giving enough time to reconcile discrepancies with counterparties and avoid unwanted risk exposure before markets moved.

Secondary objectives were consistency across the team and scalability: the solution needed to work reliably regardless of who ran it, and accommodate changes in the broker list or threshold rules without rebuilding the workflow.

03 - Analysis

A fragmented, high-risk manual process

Previously, the process was distributed across several team members, each responsible for different brokers. This structure increased the risk of inconsistencies, human error, and delayed reconciliation, exactly the wrong conditions for a process where accuracy is non-negotiable.

The main inefficiencies came from repetitive operational tasks: valuing positions, comparing exposures against thresholds, identifying breaches, and preparing counterparty communications. None of these steps added analytical value, they were execution, not thinking.

"The fragmentation was the core problem. When the same process runs differently across team members, consistency and control become impossible to guarantee."
04 - Solution

A centralised VBA tool for the full workflow

Using VBA, I developed an automated Excel-based tool that centralised and standardised the entire process, from data aggregation to counterparty communication. The solution was designed to be run each morning by any team member with consistent output.

Data
Collateral aggregation & valuation
Automatically aggregates collateral positions across all brokers and applies current market values to calculate exposure.
Risk
Exposure calculation & threshold flags
Compares collateral value against securities lent, identifies over and under collateralised positions, and flags any threshold breaches.
Operations
Margin call allocation & balancing
Calculates and allocates margin calls across brokers based on exposure positions, with a built-in rule determining which side initiates the call.
Reporting
Daily reports & draft emails
Generates daily reports for each responsible team member and prepares draft Outlook emails to counterparties when a margin call is required.

A key rule embedded in the logic was that the margin call sender depended on the direction of the exposure, ensuring consistency in communication flow regardless of who ran the tool that morning.

05 - Challenges

Robustness in a risk-sensitive environment

The main challenge was not building the tool, it was ensuring the accuracy and robustness of the data. Given the operational risk involved, even small errors could result in missed exposures or incorrect margin calls sent to counterparties.

01
Multiple validation checkpoints
Validation steps were introduced throughout the process to catch data issues before they propagated, including cross-checks between aggregated positions and source data totals.
02
Exception handling
The tool needed to handle missing data, format inconsistencies across broker files, and edge cases in threshold logic without failing silently or producing incorrect output.
03
Outlook integration
Integrating VBA with Outlook for automated draft email generation added technical complexity, particularly ensuring the correct counterparty contacts and email formats were applied consistently.
04
Workflow stability across scenarios
The tool needed to remain stable whether run on a quiet day with no margin calls or a volatile morning with multiple threshold breaches across brokers simultaneously.

Impact
Daily margin call process reduced from over one hour of fragmented manual work to approximately 15 minutes, including validation and checks, with consistent output across the team regardless of who ran it.

Beyond time savings, the process became more reliable. Operational risk was reduced by removing the inconsistencies that came from a distributed manual approach. The workflow could simply be refreshed each morning, producing the same structured output every day.

The shift from a team-distributed process to a single centralised tool also improved oversight, any threshold breach or data anomaly was immediately visible rather than potentially buried in one team member's spreadsheet.

07 - Key Takeaway
In risk-sensitive environments, automation is about control as much as efficiency
  • The value of automation here was not only time saved, but consistency and control. A process that runs the same way every morning, regardless of who runs it, is a fundamentally lower-risk process.
  • Fragmentation is a hidden operational risk. When the same workflow is executed differently across team members, inconsistencies accumulate, and in a margin call context, that inconsistency has direct financial consequences.
  • Validation matters more than speed. In a risk-sensitive environment, a fast tool that occasionally produces incorrect output is worse than a slow manual process. Building in checkpoints was not optional.
  • Outlook integration and exception handling were the hardest parts technically, not the financial logic. Understanding the workflow deeply made the implementation tractable.
