01 - Starting Point

From fencing to running

After an international fencing career, I started exploring running more seriously as a new athletic challenge. I had already completed several half marathons and trail runs, and the marathon distance had always been something I wanted to attempt.

The goal was not just performance, but curiosity and personal challenge - continuing to push limits through a different sport, with a different set of demands on the body and on discipline.

02 - Objective

Prepare a marathon around a full-time career

The objective was clear: prepare for a marathon while maintaining a full-time career in finance. Performance mattered, but it was never the only variable. The real challenge was finding the right balance across five competing demands simultaneously.

Priority 1
Full-time job in finance
Demanding role with occasional urgent priorities. Training always adapted around work - never the other way around.

03 - Planning & Process

Reverse engineering from race day

The plan was built using a reverse engineering approach - starting from race day and working backwards. This meant fixing the target date first, then structuring the preparation cycle around it rather than building forward from a starting point.

01
Set the target and work backwards
Race date fixed first. 16-week preparation block identified, preceded by a short base period to build aerobic foundation before the structured plan began.
02
Structure the weekly load
4 sessions per week built around work schedule: one long run, one tempo session, one interval session, and one easy recovery run. Strength training added at the start.
03
Set a clear pace target
Target time of 3h35 set at the start, corresponding to approximately 5:05/km pace. All training sessions calibrated around this objective from week one.
04
Build in flexibility from day one
The plan was treated as a framework, not a fixed schedule. Each week reviewed against upcoming work commitments, travel and recovery status before confirming the session structure.
04 - Adaptation During Training

Consistency over perfection

Very early in the process, strength training was reduced and eventually removed due to time and recovery constraints. The focus shifted toward prioritising key running sessions rather than trying to optimise every element of the preparation simultaneously.

Adjustments were made continuously throughout the 16 weeks:

-
Sessions were moved when work priorities required it - rescheduled to evenings, early mornings or weekends without disrupting the overall load.
-
Long runs were rescheduled when needed, with the weekly structure rebuilt around the most important sessions rather than trying to recover missed volume.
-
Some sessions were shortened based on fatigue - recognising that a shorter quality session was more valuable than a full session run below target effort.
-
Training remained flexible around travel and Canadian winter weather conditions, adjusting timing, route and format as needed.

The objective throughout was not perfection, but consistency over time. A plan executed at 85% consistently over 16 weeks delivers more than a plan executed at 100% for four weeks and then abandoned.

3h34:42 - and everything else still intact

Result
Finished the marathon in 3h34:42 - 18 seconds under the initial target of 3h35 - while maintaining balance across work, training and personal life throughout the 16-week preparation.

The main success was not the time itself, but the ability to execute the plan consistently while adapting to constraints along the way. The result was a byproduct of the process - not the other way around.

Work was never compromised. Social and personal life remained intact. The winter was navigated. And the marathon got done.

06 - Key Takeaway
Structure priorities, adapt consistently, deliver
  • Long-term performance is not about maximising every input at once, but about structuring priorities clearly and adapting consistently over time.
  • The reverse engineering approach - starting from the end goal and working backwards - forced clarity on what actually needed to happen each week, rather than optimising in isolation.
  • Dropping strength training early was the right call. Knowing when to simplify a plan to protect its core objectives is a skill in itself.
  • The ability to set ambitious goals, commit fully, and adjust when necessary is directly transferable to professional environments - particularly in roles where priorities shift and execution requires both structure and flexibility.
